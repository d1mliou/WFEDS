STAGE-C CLAIM-LABELLING PROMPT, Axis-3 structured-output experiment.

FROZEN JUDGING ARTEFACT. This file is the skeleton section 2.9 of
scripts/validation/axis3_structured_output_preregistration.md specifies: what
the judge sees and in what order, what the judge never sees, and the output
shape. Its sha256 is recorded in stage_c_full/stage_c_judge_provenance.json for
every judging pass, beside the rubric hash and the renderer hash, so the exact
instrument behind any stored label set is recoverable. Compute the three with
`python scripts/validation/stage_c_hashes.py`. Editing this file re-freezes the
experiment: labels produced under the previous hash are not poolable with labels
produced under the new one.

The {{DOUBLE_BRACE}} tokens are filled by the harness. A filled prompt must
contain no such token; a prompt that still does is a harness bug and the call
must not be made.

================================================================================
SECTION 0. YOUR TASK
================================================================================

You are labelling claims for faithfulness against a tool payload. One batch is
one call. For every record in this batch, and for every numbered claim in that
record, assign exactly one label from the five of section 2.5, using the four
boundary tests of 2.6 in strict precedence, then the tie-breaks R1 to R8 of 2.7,
then the worked examples of 2.8 as calibration.

Ground truth is the compact payload printed inside each record and nothing else.
Not your own knowledge of wildfires, not what a sensible model would probably
have been told, not what the pipeline could in principle compute. If a quantity
is not in that payload, a claim that asserts it is unsupported no matter how
plausible it is.

Judge the claim as written. Do not repair it, do not read a defensible meaning
into an indefensible sentence, and do not soften a label because the record is
otherwise good. Each claim is labelled on its own.

================================================================================
SECTION 2. WHAT THIS PROMPT WITHHOLDS, AND WHY
================================================================================

The following are deliberately absent. Do not infer them, do not ask for them,
and do not let a guess about them move a label:

  * the arm, that is whether the record came from the free baseline or from the
    structured-output intervention;
  * the model under test;
  * the case gold, that is the expected answer for this scenario;
  * the cutoff_events ground truth, the derived list of hours at which a
    settlement loses its last route;
  * the other pass, where a second labelling pass over the same record exists;
  * any prior human decision on this record or on any other;
  * any pass rate, clean rate or aggregate score, current or historical;
  * the raw JSON envelope of a structured record.

Why it matters that you not reconstruct them: these labels are the measurement,
and the comparison they feed is only meaningful if the same instrument was
applied to both arms without knowing which one it was looking at. Blinding is
not complete and the pre-registration says so: templated sentences can be
recognisable and batches are single-arm by construction. That residual is
declared. It is bounded only if you apply the rules to the text in front of you
rather than to a theory about where it came from. A phrasing you suspect is
templated gets exactly the label the same assertion in free prose would get.

================================================================================
SECTION 4. WHAT YOU RETURN
================================================================================

Return one object per record, in the order the records were given, with exactly
this shape:

{uid, claims:[{claim_id, text, label, justification}], notes}

Field by field:

  uid            the record's uid, copied exactly as printed.
  claim_id       the number from the canonical claim list, as a string. A split
                 claim keeps the parent number with a letter suffix ("7a",
                 "7b"); a merge joins the numbers with a plus ("7+8").
  text           the claim text you labelled. For a split or a merge, the text
                 as you read it; otherwise the claim verbatim.
  label          exactly one of SUPPORTED, CONTRADICTED, NOT_IN_RESULT,
                 ADVISORY_INFERENCE, LIMITATION_STATEMENT. No other string, no
                 empty value, no two labels.
  justification  ONE sentence. It must name at least one payload field, for
                 example cut_off, routed, impacted, at_risk, period,
                 edges_removed, final_front_class4_pct or weather_source, or
                 else the literal token "no field", which is reserved for a
                 NOT_IN_RESULT whose subject does not exist in the payload at
                 all. Name the rule you applied, T1 to T4 or R1 to R8, in the
                 same sentence.
  notes          anything about the record as a whole: an ambiguity you resolved,
                 a claim you split or merged and why, a rule you found
                 underdetermined. Empty string if there is nothing to say.

Every claim in the list gets an entry. Do not skip a claim you find trivial, and
do not add an entry for a claim that is not in the list.

This shape is checked deterministically after the call. A record that returns an
unknown label, a claim_id that is not in its list, a missing claim, a
justification that is not one sentence, or a justification that names neither a
payload field nor the literal token "no field", is re-queued once and then
escalated to a human with the reason judge_output_invalid. It is never silently
accepted, so a guessed label costs more than an honest "notes" entry saying the
rule was underdetermined.

Return only the objects. No preamble, no commentary outside "notes", no summary,
and no count of how many claims you labelled each way.
